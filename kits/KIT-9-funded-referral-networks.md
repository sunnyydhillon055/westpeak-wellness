# Kit 9 — Funded referral networks

**The highest-value item in this folder, and the one nobody thinks of.**

Every other kit competes for someone who is already searching for a counsellor.
These programmes send you people who have **already been funded and are looking
for anyone with availability**. There is no marketing step: the client arrives
having been told to find an RCC, and the barrier is being on the list.

A new practice with no reviews and no domain authority competes badly on search
and competes *evenly* on these, because the selection criterion is registration
and availability, not reputation.

> the exact current onboarding form for ICBC, and whether CVAP's approved-provider
> list is presently open to new applications. Everything else below was verified

## Priority order

Ranked by realistic client volume for a virtual BC practice, not by how easy
they are to join.

| # | Programme | Why it matters here | Effort |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **ICBC** | Largest single funded stream for BC counsellors | Medium |
| 2 | **Telus Health / Homewood (EAP)** | National EAP panels; steady, low-effort referrals | Medium |

## 1 · ICBC — the big one

Since the 2021 Enhanced Care changes, **anyone injured in a BC crash is
pre-approved for 12 counselling sessions with a Registered Clinical Counsellor
in the first 12 weeks, with no doctor's note required.**

Read that again, because it is unusual: the funding exists *before* the client
finds a counsellor. Someone in the days after a crash is handed a benefit and
told to find an RCC, and the constraint is almost always **availability**, not
choice. This is the one channel where a small practice competes evenly with an
established clinic.

**Why virtual is an advantage rather than a compromise here.** A recently
injured person may not be able to drive, may be in physical pain sitting in a
waiting room, and is frequently managing physio appointments in the same weeks.
Sessions from their own sofa is not the fallback option — it is often the only
workable one.

**What to do**
1. Register as an ICBC vendor/service provider through ICBC's provider channel and obtain a vendor number.
2. Set **"Serves ICBC clients"** on the BCACC Find a Counsellor listing — the directory carries this filter, and people use it. (Kit 2 covers the listing; this is one more checkbox on it.)
3. Note the same on the Psychology Today profile (Kit 3) under insurance/funding.
4. Add a short `/resources` page on the site once accepted, so the claim is verifiable rather than asserted.

**Do not skip step 4.** Naming a funder you are not actually registered with is
the fastest way to have an awkward first session and a refused invoice.

## 2 · Employee assistance programmes

EAP panels are the steadiest low-effort referral source in Canadian private
practice. Rates are lower than the private fee — usually meaningfully lower —
and the trade is volume and zero acquisition cost.

**Apply to, in this order:**

- **Telus Health** (formerly LifeWorks / Morneau Shepell) — the largest network in Canada.
- **Homewood Health** — long-established, accepts RCCs.
- **GreenShield Health** — absorbed Inkblot; runs its own provider network.
- **CloudMD** — smaller, worth doing once the first two are in.

**What every one of them will ask for:** registration number and standing,
proof of professional liability insurance, CV, a short practice description,
languages, modalities, and availability. Assemble the pack once and reuse it —
see the standing answers below.

**Two decisions worth making before applying, not after:**

- **How many EAP slots per week you will hold.** The rate is lower, so an
  uncapped EAP intake quietly converts a practice into a low-rate one. Pick a
  number and hold it.
- **Punjabi is worth stating prominently.** EAP panels route by language, and
  Punjabi-capable providers are scarce on every one of them. This is the
  single strongest thing this practice can put on an EAP application.

## 3 · Crime Victim Assistance Program (CVAP)

BC government programme funding counselling for victims of crime, immediate
family members, and some witnesses. Providers must be **approved**, which means
an RCC in good standing meeting CVAP's standards for training and experience.

**Why it is worth the paperwork:** CVAP funds substantial courses of work
rather than a handful of sessions, the client has no fee barrier at all, and
the province is persistently short of approved providers — particularly outside
the Lower Mainland and particularly in languages other than English. A virtual
BC-wide practice with Punjabi is close to the exact gap.

**Sensitivity to hold in mind.** This is trauma work with a funding body
attached, which means a reporting relationship a private client does not have.
Worth being deliberate about capacity before joining rather than after.

**Start:** VictimLink BC, 1-800-563-0808, or the CVAP pages on gov.bc.ca.

## 4 · FNHA and NIHB

First Nations Health Authority mental-health benefits, and the federal
Non-Insured Health Benefits programme, both fund counselling for eligible
First Nations and Inuit clients, and both maintain approved-provider lists.

**Virtual is a real advantage.** Many eligible clients are in communities with
no local counsellor at all, which is the same argument the Prince George,
Kamloops and Kelowna pages make — here with the funding already attached.

**Approach this one carefully rather than quickly.** Working with Indigenous
clients under these programmes carries cultural-safety expectations that are
not a form-filling exercise, and the honest question to ask first is whether
the practice has the training and supervision to do this well. If not, that is
a reason to get it, not a reason to apply anyway.

---

## 5 · Veterans Affairs Canada, via Medavie Blue Cross

VAC funds mental-health treatment for veterans and serving members through
Medavie Blue Cross, which registers providers directly. Lower volume than the
others, but engagements are typically long and the administration is
straightforward once registered.

## Standing answers — assemble once, reuse everywhere

Every application above asks the same nine things. Fill these in once:

```
Practitioner:        Aman Bains Dhillon, MA, RCC
Designation:         Registered Clinical Counsellor
Registration:        #20111, BC Association of Clinical Counsellors
Practice:            Westpeak Wellness
Business number:     [ your GST/BN, if registered ]
Liability insurance: [ insurer, policy number, expiry ]
Service delivery:    Secure video only, clients physically in BC
Languages:           English, Punjabi
Modalities:          EMDR, CBT, Gottman-informed couples work,
                     trauma-informed practice
Populations:         Adults; couples; South Asian and first-generation adults;
                     healthcare and shift workers
Availability:        [ sessions/week you will actually hold for this panel ]
Fee (private):       $140 individual / $170 couples
Website:             https://www.westpeakwellness.com
Email:               info@westpeakwellness.com
```

**Professional liability insurance is the one that stalls applications.** Every
panel requires proof and several require a specific minimum. Have the
certificate as a PDF before starting rather than discovering it mid-form.
