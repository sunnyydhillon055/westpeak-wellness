# Job ad — Registered Clinical Counsellor

Paste-ready for Indeed, the BCACC job board, CounsellingBC, or LinkedIn.

**Terms:** 70/30 split in the counsellor's favour · $95–$120 per **clinical**
hour · 5–25 hours per week · flexible · fully remote · **independent
contractor**.

The two numbers line up exactly, which is worth knowing when someone asks:

| Session | Fee | 70% to the counsellor |
|---|---|---|
| Individual, 50 min | $140 | **$98** |
| Couples, 50 min | $170 | **$119** |
| EMDR intensive, 90 min | $190 | $133 (≈$89/hr over 90 min) |

So the advertised $95–$120 per clinical hour *is* the 70/30 split across the
two core services. Both are stated in the ad — the range because BC's Pay
Transparency Act has required a wage range on every publicly advertised posting
since 1 November 2023, and the percentage because publishing the actual split
is one of the few things that makes counsellors trust a job ad on sight.
([Province of BC guidance](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/governments/about-the-bc-government/gender-equity/wage-or-salary-information-on-job-postings))

Written in the first person throughout. For a one-person practice hiring its
first counsellor, "I" reads as a person you would work with; "the practice"
reads as a form. That is the single biggest lever on how many people apply.

## Registered Clinical Counsellor (RCC) — Remote, anywhere in BC

**Westpeak Wellness · Fully remote · Contract**
**70% of every session fee — $95–$120 per clinical hour · 5–25 hrs/week, you choose**

### Hello

I run a virtual counselling practice serving adults across British Columbia,
and I have reached the point where I am turning people away. That is the reason
this post exists, and I would rather hand those clients to someone good than
keep stretching my own calendar.

So: I am looking for one Registered Clinical Counsellor to take referrals
alongside me. Fully remote, hours entirely your own, and no expectation that
you make this your whole working life unless you want to.

### What the work is

Adults, by secure video, anywhere in the province. Anxiety, depression, trauma,
burnout, relationship difficulty, life transitions — the ordinary, difficult
range of a general adult practice. Couples work too, if that is something you
do well and enjoy.

There is no office and no catchment area. A client in Prince George gets the
same access as one in Vancouver, which is most of the reason I built it this
way.

### What you would *not* be doing

I think this list matters more than a duties list, so it goes first:

- **You do not find your own clients.** Referrals come to you. That is the
  whole arrangement
- **No invoicing, no chasing payment, no booking admin.** Payment is taken when
  the client books and receipts issue automatically. You will never send an
  invoice or ask anyone for money
- **No overhead.** No lease, no platform fees, no software subscription,
  nothing deducted for "marketing" or "admin". The 70/30 split is the whole
  arrangement and there is nothing underneath it
- **No minimum caseload, no quota, no pressure to upsell packages**
- **No crisis line, no on-call, no after-hours.** This is a scheduled practice
  and it says so to clients plainly

### Hours — genuinely flexible

**Between 5 and 25 hours a week, and you set them.**

That range is wide on purpose. It means you can start with four or five clients
while keeping other work, and grow only if you want to. It also means a
permanently part-time caseload is a perfectly good answer — if you tell me you
want six hours a week forever, that is a fine thing to want and it will not
count against you.

Evenings are the most useful, because that is when most people ask to be seen.
If you can offer some, say so.

### The honest part

**You would be the first counsellor to join me,** and I would rather say what
that means than let you find out.

The downside is real: there is no existing team, no ready-made peer group, and
some of how this works we will figure out together rather than me handing you a
manual.

The upside is also real. Your caseload builds at your pace rather than landing
on you at full volume. You have actual say in how this role takes shape,
because you are the one shaping it. And you will not be the thirtieth face on a
directory page — clients will know who you are.

I am not going to pretend this suits everybody. If you want a finished system
to step into, this is not that, and I would rather you knew now.

### What I am looking for

**Needed:**

- Current registration as a **Registered Clinical Counsellor (RCC)** with the
  BC Association of Clinical Counsellors, in good standing
- Your own professional liability insurance
- Real comfort working by video — not "willing to give it a go"
- A private, quiet, professional space and a connection that will not drop
  halfway through a session
- The judgement to work independently, and to know when to check something with
  someone

**Would be great, but genuinely not required:**

- EMDR training, at any stage from Part 1
- Gottman Method training, or other structured couples work
- Some evening availability
- Experience with perinatal mental health, men's mental health, or with
  healthcare and shift workers

**Newly registered? Please apply.** If you have just finished your hours and
you are good at this, I am interested. A quiet, well-supported start with a
manageable caseload is not a bad way to begin, and it is close to the best
thing I can offer.

**Not needed at all:** hospital experience, work with children or adolescents,
medical terminology, or a research background. This is an adult outpatient
practice and none of that is what the work asks for.

### How to apply

Email **info@westpeakwellness.com** with:

1. Your CV — plain is fine
2. Your BCACC registration number
3. A few paragraphs on **how you actually work**. Not a modalities list. How you
   decide what to use, and what you do when something is not working. That is
   the part I will read twice
4. Roughly how many hours a week you are after, and whether you can do evenings

**No formal cover letter required.** An honest email is better than a polished
one, and I would rather hear how you think than read something you wrote for
someone else's posting.

I read every application myself and I reply to all of them, including the ones
that are a no. Being left to wonder is a rubbish way to be treated and I am not
going to do it.

If you are on the fence about whether you are a fit, send it anyway. The worst
outcome is a friendly no.

Counsellors of all backgrounds are welcome here. If any part of this process
needs adjusting for you, just say so in your email — it is not a problem and it
will not be held against you.

## Notes for posting

**Where to post, best return first:**

1. **[BCACC job board](https://bcacc.ca/)** — members only, so every applicant
   is already an RCC. By far the best signal-to-noise
2. **[CounsellingBC](https://www.counsellingbc.com/)** — BC-specific, low cost
3. **LinkedIn** — good reach among newly-registered RCCs
4. **UBC / SFU / Adler / City University counselling alumni lists** — where
   people who just finished their hours actually look. Given the ad explicitly
   welcomes new registrants, this may be your best channel
5. **Indeed** — widest reach, lowest signal. Expect volume, most of it wrong

**What makes this ad likely to get replies.** Worth keeping if you edit it:
the split is published as a number rather than "competitive compensation"; the
"what you would *not* do" list comes before the duties; the downside of being
first is stated instead of hidden; new registrants are explicitly invited; and
no cover letter is demanded. Counsellors read a lot of vague postings — being
specific is the differentiator.

**Two lines to keep exactly as written.** "Nothing else deducted" and "I reply
to all of them" are both promises. The first is what stops an applicant
assuming a hidden admin fee; the second is unusual enough that people notice.
Only keep them if you will honour them — an unanswered applicant in a
profession this small talks to other counsellors.

**Contractor status is not settled by what you call it.** If you set someone's
hours, require exclusivity, or direct how they practise clinically, the CRA may
treat them as an employee whatever the agreement says. What the ad describes —
self-set availability, their own insurance and supervision, free to work
elsewhere — is what keeps it genuine. Ten minutes with an accountant before the
first person signs is worth it.

**Have an answer ready for "is the 70% before or after payment processing?"**
Someone will ask on day one. Stripe takes roughly 2.9% + 30¢, so on a $140
session that is about $4.36. Decide whether the 70% comes off the gross $140
($98) or the net (~$94.95) and say so consistently. Off the gross is simpler,
more generous, and easier to defend — and the difference is about $3 a session.

**If you later want a Punjabi-speaking counsellor specifically,** tell me and I
will write that variant. It is deliberately absent here, since you asked for a
general RCC post and adding it would narrow the pool for no benefit.
