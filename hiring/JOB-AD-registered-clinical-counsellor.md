**Two things to settle before posting** — both marked `«LIKE THIS»` in the ad:

1. **Is the rate per *clinical* hour or per hour *worked*?** The ad says
   clinical hour, which is the standard in private practice and what a
   counsellor will assume. If you mean every hour including note-writing,
   intake admin and no-shows, your cost per session rises materially. Decide
   this now — it is the first thing a serious applicant will ask.
2. **Contractor or employee.** Drafted as contractor, which fits self-set
   availability and a variable 5–25 hours. If you want an employee, say so and
   I will redraft: it changes tax, insurance, supervision and control language,
   not just a word.

## Registered Clinical Counsellor (RCC) — Remote, BC-wide

**Westpeak Wellness · Remote (anywhere in British Columbia)**
**$95–$120 per clinical hour · 5–25 hours per week · flexible schedule**

### About the practice

Westpeak Wellness is a virtual counselling practice serving adults across
British Columbia. Every session runs by secure video — there is no office, no
commute, and no catchment area. A client in Prince George has the same access
as a client in Vancouver.

The work covers anxiety, depression, trauma, burnout and relationship
difficulty, using EMDR, CBT and Gottman-informed couples therapy. A particular
focus is cultural context: sessions are offered in Punjabi as well as English,
and the practice's clinical research examined intergenerational trauma in the
South Asian community. Clients do not arrive having to explain family
expectation, obligation or generational silence from scratch.

Demand has outgrown what one counsellor can hold well, which is why this role
exists.

### The role, honestly

**You would be the first counsellor to join the practice.** That is the real
trade-off and it is better said plainly than dressed up.

What it costs you: there is no existing team and no ready-made peer group, and
some of how this works will be built together rather than handed over.

What it gets you: a caseload that grows at your pace instead of being inherited
at full volume, real influence over how the role is structured, and a practice
where you are not the thirtieth name on a directory page.

If you are looking for a finished system to slot into, this is not it. If you
want genuine flexibility and a say in how it develops, it may be exactly right.

### Hours

**5 to 25 hours a week, and you set them.** The range is deliberate: it means
you can start at four or five clients while keeping other work, and scale up as
referrals build without renegotiating anything. It also means this suits
someone who wants a defined part-time caseload permanently, and that is a
perfectly good answer — say so and it will not count against you.

**Evening availability is the most useful.** That is when most enquiries ask to
be seen, and it is the practice's tightest constraint.

### What you would do

- Provide individual counselling to adults across BC by secure video
- Provide couples counselling, if that is within your competence and interest
- Run free 15-minute intake consultations and judge fit honestly — including
  saying no and referring on when you are not the right person
- Keep clinical records to BCACC standards in Cliniko, where scheduling,
  records and invoicing all live
- Work within your scope, and name it clearly when something falls outside it

### What you would not do

Being specific here is more useful than a long list of duties:

- **No finding your own clients.** Referrals come to you.
- **No invoicing, no chasing payment, no booking admin.** Payment is taken at
  booking; receipts issue automatically.
- **No overhead.** No lease, no platform fees, no software costs.
- **No minimum caseload.** You take what you can hold well.
- **No in-person work, no crisis line, no on-call.** This is a scheduled
  practice and it tells clients plainly that it is not a crisis service.

### What we are looking for

**Required:**

- Current registration as a **Registered Clinical Counsellor (RCC)** with the
  BC Association of Clinical Counsellors, in good standing
- Your own professional liability insurance
- Real competence delivering therapy by video — not "open to trying it"
- A private, confidential, professional space to work from, and a connection
  that will not drop mid-session
- The judgement to work independently and to know when to consult

**Any of these would matter a great deal:**

- **Fluency in Punjabi.** This is the practice's clearest gap and the reason a
  large share of enquiries arrive. If you can hold a session in Punjabi, put it
  in the first line of your email
- EMDR training, at any stage from Part 1 onward
- Gottman Method training for couples work
- **Evening availability**
- Experience with South Asian clients, or with first- and second-generation
  clients navigating family expectation and cultural identity
- Experience in perinatal mental health, men's mental health, or with
  healthcare and shift workers

**We are not asking for** hospital experience, work with children or
adolescents, medical terminology, or a research background. This is an adult
outpatient practice and none of that is what the work needs.

### How to apply

Email **info@westpeakwellness.com** with:

1. Your CV
2. Your BCACC registration number
3. A short note — a few paragraphs is plenty — on **how you actually work**.
   Not a modalities list; how you decide what to use, and what you do when

4. Your availability, including whether you can offer evenings, and roughly how

unsuccessful ones.

Westpeak Wellness welcomes applications from counsellors of all backgrounds. If
any part of this process needs adjusting for you, say so in your email and it
will be accommodated.

## Notes for posting

**Where to post, best return first:**

1. **[BCACC job board](https://bcacc.ca/)** — members only, so every applicant
   is already an RCC. Highest signal-to-noise by a distance
2. **[CounsellingBC](https://www.counsellingbc.com/)** — BC-specific
3. **LinkedIn** — the best channel for the Punjabi-speaking angle specifically
4. **Indeed** — widest reach, lowest signal. Expect volume, most of it wrong
5. **UBC / SFU / Adler / City University counselling alumni lists** — where
   newly-registered RCCs actually look

**On the Punjabi line.** Written as a strong asset, not a requirement, on
purpose. Making it mandatory raises a bona fide occupational requirement
question you do not need, and shrinks an already thin pool. As written it still
brings the right applicants to the top.

**What to expect.** Punjabi-speaking RCCs are genuinely scarce in BC — which is
exactly why the practice has the demand it does. Budget six to twelve weeks.
Consider hiring a strong candidate who does not speak Punjabi: they can absorb
general caseload and free up your Punjabi-language capacity, which is worth
more than holding out.

**Employment status is not decided by what you call it.** If you set someone's
hours, require exclusivity, or direct how they practise clinically, the CRA may
treat them as an employee whatever the contract says. Self-set availability,
their own insurance and their own supervision — as above — is what keeps a
contractor arrangement genuine. Worth ten minutes with an accountant before the
first person signs.

**The $95–$120 range needs a rule.** Decide now what puts someone at the top:
EMDR certification, Punjabi fluency, and couples competence are the three that
actually raise what a counsellor is worth to *this* practice. Deciding it in
advance keeps the range defensible under the Pay Transparency Act, and stops
the first negotiation from setting the precedent for everyone after.
