# Nurture sequence — three emails

For people who downloaded the coverage checklist from `/pricing`. Load into
whatever tool you use; the copy is written to be pasted as-is.

---

## Before you send anything

**These go only to people who asked for the guide.** Not to clients, not to
consultation enquiries, not to anyone whose address arrived another way. A
counselling client receiving marketing email because they once booked a session
is a boundary problem, not a growth tactic.

**Every email needs a working unsubscribe link.** Required under CASL, and one
click, not a preference centre.

**Nothing here promises an outcome.** No "you'll feel better", no success rates,
no implication that therapy resolves anything on a schedule. BCACC advertising
standards apply to email exactly as they do to the site.

**Send them slowly.** Day 0, day 4, day 11. Somebody who downloaded a coverage
checklist is deciding whether to spend money on therapy, not waiting to be
sold to.

## Notes for whoever maintains this

- **Three emails, then stop.** No indefinite newsletter. The value was delivered
  in email one; the rest is context. A sequence that never ends teaches people
  to ignore you.
- **If someone books, remove them from the sequence.** Continuing to send
  marketing email to a new client is the fastest way to make a good first
  session awkward.
- **Do not add a fourth with a discount or a deadline.** Scarcity tactics on
  mental-health care are unpleasant and, for a regulated practice, a genuine
  compliance risk.
- **Not a crisis channel.** If somebody replies in distress, respond as a person
  and point at 9-8-8 and 310-6789. Automated sequences must never be the only
  thing that answers.
