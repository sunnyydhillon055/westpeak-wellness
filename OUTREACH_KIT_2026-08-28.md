# Outreach kit — every send is written; the owner adds a name and presses go

**Written:** 28 August 2026. Items 32–36 and 41 of `FIFTY_MORE_2026-08-28.md`.
Everything below is copy-paste ready and BCACC-compliant: no outcome claims, no
testimonials, no statistics invented for effect. Facts about the practice come from
the reference block in `OFFSITE_KIT_2026-08-27.md`; do not vary the wording of the
business name.

> **One rule for all outreach:** every message offers something useful to the
> recipient's audience, not a favour to this practice. Link earned that way survives
> editor turnover and algorithm updates; link begged for does not.

## 2 · Pitch: the 2027 regulation change (The Tyee / Daily Hive / Fraser Valley Current)

Subject: **In 2027, "counsellor" finally becomes a regulated title in BC — most people have no idea**

> Hi [name] — right now, anyone in BC can legally call themselves a counsellor or
> therapist: no degree, no oversight, no complaints process. That ends on
> 29 November 2027, when psychotherapy comes under the College of Health and Care
> Professionals of BC. Almost nobody outside the profession knows this is coming,
> and the practical questions write themselves: how do you check a counsellor
> *today*, what changes for people already in therapy, and what happened in the
> provinces that regulated first?
>
> I'm a Registered Clinical Counsellor in the Fraser Valley and can walk through it
> in plain language, including the two-minute register check anyone can do now.
> A explainer on my site covers the ground if useful background helps:
> westpeakwellness.com/resources/what-is-a-registered-clinical-counsellor
>
> — Aman Bains Dhillon, MA, RCC (BCACC #20111)
